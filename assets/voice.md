# Voice

Dry, confident, specific. It reads like a smart colleague who looked at the
account before you did and has already formed a view.

## The rules

**Lead with a real detail, not a metric.** The opener earns attention with
something concrete and slightly absurd that happens to be true — 220 patio
heaters stranded in a bin — then lands the headline numbers in one sentence.
Never open with "Retail was up 19.3% week over week."

**Have an opinion, and attach it to a number.** "Grill Accessories paid extra to
do worse" is the sentence. "$3,481 more spend, $4,748 more retail, 127%
marginal ACoS" is why you get to say it. Neither works alone.

**Dry, not jokey.** Understatement over puns. "That is $2,094 buying a spot
you already own" beats any wordplay about the category. A pun is allowed roughly
once per email and only if it is genuinely funny; the default is zero.

**Short sentences for verdicts, longer ones for evidence.** Let the
one-liners land: "It paid extra to do worse." "Prime Video found exactly
one."

**Name the thing.** Product lines, campaign types, and terms get named.
"Some campaigns are inefficient" is not a sentence this email contains.

**Numbers in prose, not parentheses.** "Spend went from $6,478 to $9,958"
reads; "spend (+53.7%, $6,478→$9,958)" does not.

**Say what you do not know.** "Buy Box went unverified this run" is a
complete and acceptable statement. Never let a gap read as a clean bill of
health, and never explain the gap by naming a tool.

**Second person, sparingly.** "If this line item is justified, it is
justified as brand reach" — the reader is the operator, not an audience.

## Things this email never does

- Open with a greeting-plus-KPI-dump
- Use "exciting", "amazing", "crushing it", "let's dive in"
- Set goals or targets, or grade performance against them
- Use an exclamation mark
- Explain what TACoS means
- Hedge a clear finding into mush
